# Code of Conduct

## The short version

This project exists because a body of music matters to people. Argue about the music as much as
you like. Don't be contemptuous of the people.

## What we're asking for

**Assume the other person knows something you don't.** A retired music teacher in Santiniketan who
has never opened a terminal, and a machine-learning researcher who has never heard a khemta, are
both essential here and neither outranks the other. Explain your field's basics without
condescension, and ask about theirs without embarrassment.

**Disagree about the notation freely.** Whether a phrase carries komal Ga, whether a matra is
divided in two or three, whether an edition is reliable — these are the substance of the project.
Bring your source, say how sure you are, and let the evidence settle it.

**Write in the language you think in.** Bengali, Hindi, and English are all welcome in issues and
discussions. Nobody should have to translate their expertise into a second language before it
counts. If you can help translate a thread for someone, that's a real contribution.

**Uncertainty is not weakness.** "I think this is wrong but I'm not sure" is a valuable issue.
Anyone who treats honest hedging as incompetence is the problem, not the hedger.

## Not acceptable

Harassment of any kind; demeaning remarks about someone's caste, religion, region, language,
gender, sexuality, disability, age, or nationality; sexualised language or attention; publishing
others' private information; sustained disruption of discussion; using this project to push a
political line about the music's culture at the expense of the people participating in it.

## Scope

This applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, discussions, commits, reviews — and
anywhere someone is representing the project publicly.

## Enforcement

Report anything that needs attention to **indranilbanerjee21@gmail.com**. Reports are handled
privately; the reporter's identity is not shared without their consent.

The maintainer will respond as fairly and promptly as one person can, and may warn, edit or remove
contributions, or block accounts, depending on severity. If you think a decision was wrong, say so
by email — being told you got something wrong applies to maintainers too.
